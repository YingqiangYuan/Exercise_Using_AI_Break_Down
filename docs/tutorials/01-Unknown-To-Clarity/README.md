## 📖 The Core Method: Three-Part Information Breakdown

When most people hit something they don't understand, their first instinct is to dump the entire document into AI and ask: "What does this mean?"

That'll get you *an* answer, but usually not **the answer you actually need**.

Why? Because AI doesn't know:

- Who you are
- What situation you're in
- What you're trying to accomplish

**The key to getting real value from AI is to pause before you ask — and give it the context it needs.**

### The Three Parts

Whenever you're facing a document, email, or web page you can't make sense of, prepare these three types of information before handing it to AI:

---

**Part 1: Your Context**

> "Who am I, and why is this landing on my desk?"

- Your role: Are you a student? A new hire? Someone renting in the US for the first time?
- Your situation: What are you applying for? What process are you in the middle of?
- Your relationship to this document: Do you need to respond? Sign something? Or is it just informational?

**Example:**

- ❌ "What does this lease agreement mean?"
- ✅ "I'm an international student who just arrived in California, and I'm about to sign my first-ever lease. The landlord sent me this 15-page Lease Agreement, and I need to decide within 3 days whether to sign."

---

**Part 2: Your Goal**

> "What exactly do I need AI to help me with?"

Different goals produce completely different answers. Common ones include:

| Goal Type<br> | What You're Asking AI to Do<br> |
| --- | --- |
| **Understand**<br> | Explain what this document says and highlight the key points<br> |
| **Research**<br> | Analyze the pros, cons, and red flags based on this document<br> |
| **Check**<br> | Review a planned action for gaps or mistakes<br> |
| **Act**<br> | Draft a reply, fill out a form, or create a preparation checklist<br> |
Often you'll have more than one goal — that's perfectly normal. Just break it into steps:

**Example:**

- "Step 1: Help me understand the key clauses in this lease"
- "Step 2: Flag any clauses that could work against me and tell me what to watch out for"
- "Step 3: Give me a checklist of questions to confirm before I sign"

---

**Part 3: Supporting Details**

> "What else would help AI give me a sharper answer?"

These are the details you often have to think a bit harder to surface, but they can dramatically improve the quality of AI's response:

- Your budget constraints
- Your time constraints
- Personal preferences or special circumstances
- What you already know or have already tried
- Other related documents you have on hand

**Example:**

- "My budget cap is $2,000/month including utilities"
- "I'm only planning to stay for one year and might move after that"
- "I have a small dog"
- "I've read that California has tenant protection laws, but I'm not sure about the specifics"

---

### Putting It All Together: The Prompt Template

Combine all three parts, and a high-quality AI prompt looks like this:

```
[Context]
I'm [your role], currently [your situation].
I received/found [source and type of document].

[Goal]
I'd like your help with:
1. [First goal, e.g., understanding the core content of this document]
2. [Second goal, e.g., identifying risks I should watch out for]
3. [Third goal, e.g., listing the actions I need to take]

[Supporting Details]
- [Relevant personal circumstances]
- [Time/budget constraints]
- [Any other information that would help]

[Attached Document]
[Paste the content / upload a screenshot / provide a link]

```

## ⚠️ A Critical Habit: Ask AI to Cite Its Sources

This is something most people overlook, but it's incredibly important:

**Never take AI's answer at face value.**

AI sometimes "invents" plausible-sounding content or misinterprets what the original document actually says. If you don't verify, the consequences can be serious — like agreeing to unfavorable lease terms or entering the wrong numbers on your tax return.

### The Fix: Require AI to Quote from the Source

Whenever you ask AI to summarize or interpret something, **explicitly tell it to include relevant excerpts from the original document as evidence**. This lets you:

1. **Cross-check** — Compare AI's interpretation against the actual text
2. **Trace the source** — Know exactly where a conclusion came from
3. **Spot gaps** — If AI can't point to a source, it might be making things up

### Example Prompts

Add something like this to your request:

> "When answering, please quote the relevant passages from the original document and note the source (e.g., page number or section), so I can cross-verify."

> "Each time you explain a term or clause, include the exact original wording so I can compare it against the source."

> "Please cite your sources in this format: [Original, Page X] 'quoted passage'."

> "When generating a summary, include the supporting evidence from the source after each key point — I need to be able to trace everything back."

### The Difference This Makes

**Without asking for citations, AI might say:**

> The security deposit can't exceed one month's rent.

You read that, think "okay, got it," and move on — but you have no idea whether there are exceptions or what conditions apply.

**With citations, AI says:**

> The security deposit can't exceed one month's rent.
> 
> [Original, Page 35] "Beginning July 1, 2024, landlords will be limited to an amount of no more than one month's rent that they can charge as a security deposit."
> 
> However, there's an exception — small landlords (individuals who own no more than 4 units) can charge up to two months' rent:
> 
> [Original, Page 35] "Only small landlords can charge more for a security deposit. Small landlords can charge up to two month's rent for a security deposit."

Now you can:

- Confirm AI didn't fabricate anything
- Understand the rule's nuances and exceptions
- Go directly to page 35 to read the full context

### Make This a Habit

**Every time you ask AI to interpret an important document, require it to cite the source.**

This habit will help you:

- Avoid being misled by AI hallucinations
- Truly understand the information instead of relying on secondhand interpretation
- Trace and cite original sources whenever you need to

## 💡 How to Feed Information to AI

Depending on the format, you have several options:

| Format<br> | How to Share It with AI<br> |
| --- | --- |
| **Public web link**<br> | Paste the URL and let AI read it directly (if it has web access)<br> |
| **PDF / Word document**<br> | Upload the file (if AI supports file uploads)<br> |
| **Email / short text**<br> | Copy and paste it straight into the chat<br> |
| **Image / screenshot**<br> | Upload the image — AI can read text in images<br> |
| **Paper document**<br> | Take a photo and upload it<br> |
**Pro tip:** If the document is very long (say, a lease running dozens of pages), start by asking AI to "extract the key sections," then dive deeper into those sections one at a time.

### Option A: Your First Tax Season — Making Sense of a W-2

**The Setup:**

Imagine you've just finished your first year at your first full-time job. At year-end, HR hands you a W-2 form (Wage and Tax Statement) — a document you absolutely need for filing your taxes. You have no idea what all those numbers and boxes mean, but you know there's a filing deadline.

**Official Documents:**

- **W-2 Form and Employee Instructions**: [https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/fw2.pdf](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/fw2.pdf) (the back of the form has "Instructions for Employee")
- **Full Instructions Document**: [https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/iw2w3.pdf](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/iw2w3.pdf) (more detailed official guidance)

**Your Task:**

1. Open the IRS official document links above
2. Provide the link or content to AI
3. Use the "Three-Part Information Breakdown" method to get AI to help you:
- Understand what each important box on the W-2 means (Box 1, Box 2, Box 12, etc.)
- Explain which numbers matter most when you file your taxes
- Tell you what to do if any numbers are wrong
4. **Final deliverable:** Have AI generate a "W-2 Quick Reference Guide for First-Timers" (as an Artifact document), covering key terms and tax-filing tips. **Ask AI to cite source passages so you can cross-verify.**
